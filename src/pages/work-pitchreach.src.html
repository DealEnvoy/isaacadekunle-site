A football opportunity map of England. The public case study asks how incomplete research can support a decision without pretending to measure a child's opportunity.

Live beta Case study repository

Problem

{{claim:pr-beta}}

A map on its own can make a small collection look complete. A single score can make missing information look precise. The case study says the product question was to keep the research useful without that pretence.

{{claim:pr-inputs}}

What is public now

{{claim:pr-live-search}} {{claim:pr-journey-open}}

The public site, read on 15 November 2026, also says young players can browse without an account, alongside parents, guardians and coaches. The case study README describes the audience as adult parents, guardians and coaches. Both wordings are left as they stand.

The same public page says coverage is incomplete, and that a missing venue means more research is needed rather than that no football is available.

Architecture

{{claim:pr-stack-live}} {{claim:pr-arch}}

The case study separates those two lists on purpose. The checkpoint runtime is what the 7 October audit inspected. The Python and PostGIS work is described as exploration, not that backend. The application source, research registers and deployment configuration stay private. This repository is the write-up.

One decision: a zero that said too much

{{claim:pr-zero}}

The case study walks through a controlled scenario: seven researched venues, each 180 minutes away, against a 60-minute budget. An earlier screen showed a practical score of 0/100. The correction keeps the sample count and withholds the broader score. On 7 October 2026 an assistant ran a deterministic offline fixture against the earlier source and the corrected versions. The earlier source failed the assertion. The corrected versions passed. No journey provider was called for that fixture.

Source: docs/decisions-and-development.md in the case study repository.
